In short, the microservice architectural style is an approach to developing a single application as a suite of small services, each running in its own process and communicating with lightweight mechanisms, often an HTTP resource API. The services are built around business capabilities and independently deployable by fully automated deployment machinery.
There is a bare minimum of centralized management of these services, which may be written in different programming languages and use different data storage technologies.
Business capabilities refer to the collective ability of an organization to deliver value through its competencies, processes, and resources. Essentially, they are what a business can do to achieve its goals and objectives. Business capabilities encompass a range of elements including skills, technologies, processes, and organizational structures that enable a company to operate effectively and competitiyely in the market.
Here are some key aspects of business capabilities:
1.    **skills and Expertise**: The knowledge and abilities possessed by employees and teams within the organization.
2.    **Processes**: The methods and workflows used to accomplish tasks and deliver products or services.
3.    **Technologies**: The tools and systems that support business operations, including software, hardware, and digital platforms.
4.    **Resources**: The tangible and intangible assets the business can leverage, such as financial capital, intellectual property, and physical infrastructure.
5.    **Organizational Structures**: The way in which the business is organized, including its hierarchy, roles, and communication channels.
Business capabilities are often mapped and analyzed as part of strategic planning to identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. By understanding their capabilities, organizations can make informed decisions about where to invest, what markets to enter, and how to optimize their operations for better performance.
