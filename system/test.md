SUT stands for "System Under Test." It is a term commonly used in software testing to refer to the specifie system, component, or application that is being tested. The SUT is the focus of the testing activities and is subjected to various forms of testing to ensure it meets specified requirements and functions as expected.
Contexts Where SUT is Used:
Unit Testing: In unit testing, the SUT is typically a single function or method. The goal is to verify that this smallest piece of code behaves correctly in isolation.
Integration Testing: Here, the SUT might be a combination of components or modules working together. The focus is on testing the interactions between these components.
System Testing: In this context, the SUT is the entire application or system. The goal is to validate the system's compliance with specified requirements.
Acceptance Testing: The SUT is again the entire application or system, but the focus is on verifying that it meets business needs and is ready for deployment.
Performance Testing: The SUT is tested for its performance characteristics, such as speed, scalability, and stability under load.
In all these contexts, identifying the SUT is crucial as it defines the scope and boundaries of the testing to be conducted. It helps testers know what inputs to provide, what outputs to expect, and what aspects of the system's behavior are relevant to the test at hand.
